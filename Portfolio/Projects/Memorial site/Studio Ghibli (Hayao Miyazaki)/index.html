Hayao Miyazaki (宮崎 駿) • Studio Ghibli
1941 – PRESENT • MASTER ANIMATOR & STORYTELLER

Hayao Miyazaki 宮崎 駿

Co-Founder of Studio Ghibli & Pioneer of Hand-Crafted World Cinema

The creative soul behind the most beloved animated films ever made. Celebrated for breathtaking pastoral watercolors, soaring flight sequences, reverent environmentalism, and deeply human heroines, Miyazaki transformed animated filmmaking into timeless, universal art.

"The future is not a straight line. There are many different pathways to another kind of future, where heart and nature can connect."

— Hayao Miyazaki (宮崎 駿)

Landmark Cinematic Masterworks

1988

My Neighbor Totoro

となりのトトロ

A gentle, heartwarming pastoral poem celebrating childhood innocence, rural camaraderie, and the benevolent spirit of the ancient camphor tree that became Ghibli's worldwide mascot.

1997

Princess Mononoke

もののけ姫

A colossal, morally intricate fantasy epic depicting the tragic conflict between human industrial advancement and primordial forest gods, shattering Japanese box-office records.

2001

Spirited Away

千と千尋の神隠し

A dazzling mythological journey through an otherworldly spirit bathhouse that earned monumental worldwide praise, winning the 2003 Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

2023

The Boy and the Heron

君たちはどう生きるか

A deeply personal, semi-autobiographical visual meditation exploring grief, friendship, and artistic creation, winning Miyazaki his historic second Academy Award in 2024.

The Ghibli Journey & Artistic Ethos

Birth & Fascination with Flight: Born in Tokyo on 5 January 1941. His family owned Miyazaki Airplane during wartime, igniting a lifelong obsession with aviation mechanics and fantastical flying vessels seen in nearly every film.
Education & Children's Literature: Studied political science and economics at Gakushuin University, graduating in 1963 while intensely immersed in European and Japanese children's literature, shaping his narrative worldview.
Founding of Studio Ghibli (1985): Following the historic impact of Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind (1984), Miyazaki co-founded Studio Ghibli alongside mentor Isao Takahata and producer Toshio Suzuki, naming it after the Italian wind for 'hot Sahara breeze'.
Partnership with Joe Hisaishi: One of the greatest director-composer pairings in cinema history. Joe Hisaishi's evocative, stirring orchestral motifs gave emotional voice to Ghibli's skies, forests, and nostalgic landscapes.
Hand-Crafted Cel Animation Craftsmanship: Miyazaki refuses automated short-cuts, believing that pencils and physical watercolor brushes impart a warmth impossible to duplicate. He famously reviews and hand-corrects thousands of key animation frames personally.
Ecological & Humanist Themes: His narratives celebrate strong, self-reliant young female heroines, peaceful reconciliation over bloodshed, and profound reverence for the living natural earth (Shinto animism and environmentalism).
Family & Continuing Artistry: Married animator Akemi Yamaguchi in 1965. Their son Goro Miyazaki directed From Up on Poppy Hill, while Keisuke Miyazaki is a distinguished woodblock printmaker.
Endless Creative Vitality: Despite numerous announcements of retirement, his creative flame remains unquenchable, continuing to sketch storyboards and produce exclusive short animations for the Ghibli Museum in Mitaka.

Two Academy Awards & Global Cinematic Immortality

Hayao Miyazaki is universally acknowledged as a titan of cinema. He is the only director to win the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature twice for non-English productions (Spirited Away in 2003 and The Boy and the Heron in 2024), received the Academy Honorary Award (2014) for lifetime achievement, and was inducted into the Science Fiction Hall of Fame.
